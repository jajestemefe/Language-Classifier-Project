Turkey has coastal plains, a high central plateau, and various mountain ranges with rising elevation eastwards. Turkey's climate is diverse, ranging from Mediterranean and other temperate climates to semi-arid and continental types. Home to three biodiversity hotspots, Turkey is prone to frequent earthquakes and is highly vulnerable to climate change.[25] Turkey has a universal healthcare system, growing access to education, and increasing levels of innovativeness.[26] It is a leading TV content exporter.[27] With numerous UNESCO World Heritage sites and intangible cultural heritage inscriptions, and a rich and diverse cuisine, Turkey is the fourth most visited country in the world.
Turchia, meaning "the land of the Turks", had begun to be used in European texts for Anatolia by the end of the 12th century.[28][29][30] As a word in Turkic languages, Turk may mean "strong, strength, ripe" or "flourishing, in full strength".[31] It may also mean ripe as in for a fruit or "in the prime of life, young, and vigorous" for a person.[32] As an ethnonym, the etymology is still unknown.[33] In addition to usage in languages such as Chinese in the 6th century,[30] the earliest mention of Turk (𐱅𐰇𐰺𐰜, türü̲k̲; or 𐱅𐰇𐰼𐰚, türk/tẄrk) in Turkic languages comes from the Second Turkic Khaganate.[34]
