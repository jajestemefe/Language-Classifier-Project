Anatolia's historical records start with clay tablets from approximately around 2000 BC that were found in modern-day Kültepe.[55] These tablets belonged to an Assyrian trade colony.[55] The languages in Anatolia at that time included Hattian, Hurrian, Hittite, Luwian, and Palaic.[56] Hattian was a language indigenous to Anatolia, with no known modern-day connections.[56][57] Hurrian language was used in northern Syria.[56] Hittite, Palaic, and Luwian languages were "the oldest written Indo-European languages",[58] forming the Anatolian sub-group.[59][c]

Hattian rulers were gradually replaced by Hittite rulers.[55] The Hittite kingdom was a large kingdom in Central Anatolia, with its capital of Hattusa.[55] It co-existed in Anatolia with Palaians and Luwians, approximately between 1700 and 1200 BC.[55] As the Hittite kingdom was disintegrating, further waves of Indo-European peoples migrated from southeastern Europe, which was followed by warfare.[63] The Thracians were also present in modern-day Turkish Thrace.[64] It is not known if the Trojan War is based on historical events.[65] Troy's Late Bronze Age layers matches most with Iliad's story.[66]
